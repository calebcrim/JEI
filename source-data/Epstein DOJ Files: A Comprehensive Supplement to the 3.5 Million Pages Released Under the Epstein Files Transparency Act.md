# Epstein DOJ files: a comprehensive supplement to your research

**The DOJ's "Epstein Library" released 3.5 million pages across 12 datasets by January 30, 2026 — yet only half of the 6 million identified pages have been made public.** The releases have already triggered three arrests (Prince Andrew, Peter Mandelson, Thorbjørn Jagland), multiple resignations, and ongoing congressional investigations. This report catalogs specific documents, named individuals, financial trails, and investigative threads not typically covered in existing compilations, with direct URLs and EFTA document references where available.

The Epstein Files Transparency Act, signed November 19, 2025, mandated release of all unclassified records within 30 days. The DOJ complied in waves: Datasets 1–8 on December 19–22, 2025, and Datasets 9–12 on January 30, 2026. The material spans FBI 302 interviews, emails, images, videos, financial ledgers, flight manifests, and court records from five primary sources — the Florida and New York Epstein cases, the Maxwell prosecution, death investigations, a former butler investigation, and multiple FBI inquiries. Community researchers have built extensive tools to navigate these files, including JMail (jmail.world), which has cataloged **1,412,250 files** and drawn 25 million visitors.

## The 12 DOJ datasets and what each contains

**Datasets 1–7** (released December 19, 2025) contain the bulk of FBI "302" interview summaries and Palm Beach police reports from the 2005–2008 investigations. Files are numbered sequentially (EFTA00000001 through EFTA00008528+) with non-descriptive filenames. Key discoveries include a previously undisclosed **1996 FBI report** accusing Epstein of child pornography, an original **draft indictment from the 2005 Florida case**, and photos of Bill Clinton in a hot tub, on an airplane with Michael Jackson and Diana Ross, and wearing cultural garb. An internal FBI document from August 2019 lists 9 persons as family/associates and 8 as "co-conspirators" (mostly redacted except Maxwell and Brunel). Emails show investigators discussed **"10 co-conspirators"** before Epstein's July 2019 arrest, and a 2020 prosecutor email notes Trump flew on Epstein's jet "many more times" than previously known.

**Dataset 8** (released December 22, 2025, 10.67 GB uncompressed) includes prison CCTV footage, grand jury testimony from 2007 and 2019, the expired Austrian passport under the alias **"Marius Robert Fortelni"** with a Saudi Arabian residence, and a fake letter purportedly from Epstein to Larry Nassar (confirmed fabricated by FBI). It also contains call logs, a photo of Trump and Maxwell found on Steve Bannon's iPhone, and visa application records where Epstein served as point of contact for non-immigrant visa applicants from Poland, Belarus, Ukraine, Russia, China, Croatia, and Slovakia (2004–2005).

**Dataset 9** (~533,786 files, **179 GB**, known to be **incomplete at source**) is the email evidence trove. It contains private correspondence between Epstein and high-profile individuals, plus internal DOJ correspondence regarding the 2008 non-prosecution agreement. Key emails include Epstein-to-Elon Musk exchanges from December 2013 with a Google Calendar entry reading "ELON MUSK TO ISLAND DEC. 6TH"; emails with Bill Gates dating to 2013 including two unsent draft emails containing unverified allegations about Gates's personal conduct; Peter Mandelson emails suggesting he leaked market-sensitive UK government information during the 2008 financial crisis; Crown Princess Mette-Marit of Norway's post-conviction communications with Epstein; and correspondence with Ehud Barak including 2017 plans to stay at Epstein's New York residence. Community researchers discovered the DOJ pagination of Dataset 9 operates as an "infinite loop" — pages beyond approximately 13,000 contain no new documents.

**Dataset 10** (~50,403 files, **79 GB**) holds 180,000 images and 2,000+ videos seized from Epstein's properties, including approximately **14 hours of footage Epstein recorded himself**. Content includes videos of young women dancing and lying in bed (faces/bodies redacted), drone footage of Little St. James, Epstein's passport with sex offender designation, and surveillance footage stills. The DOJ initially published dozens of unredacted nude images that were later removed after the New York Times flagged them. Approximately **10.1 GB of video files** (EFTA00276494, EFTA01244748, EFTA01244749) were removed from the DOJ website and remain missing according to community trackers.

**Dataset 11** (~331,655 files, **26 GB**) contains financial ledgers, flight manifests to the U.S. Virgin Islands, property seizure records, bank statements, wire transfer records, Little St. James logbooks, boat trip logs, island blueprints, property photographs, and Ghislaine Maxwell financial records including UBS account wiring instructions.

**Dataset 12** (~154 files, 114 MB) is a supplemental late-production set requiring additional legal review, downloadable directly at justice.gov/epstein/files/DataSet%2012.zip.

The DOJ also hosts a **Court Records** section (justice.gov/epstein/court-records) with documents organized by case, including **Bryant v. Indyke**, **C.L. v. Epstein**, **Davies v. Indyke**, **Doe 17 v. Indyke**, **Doe 1000 v. Indyke**, the SDNY criminal case, and Giuffre v. Maxwell. A separate FOIA section and House Disclosures section link to congressional releases. No official DOJ index exists; the search tool at justice.gov/epstein/search provides the only built-in navigation.

## Named individuals with major file presence

### Les Wexner: the billion-dollar relationship

Wexner's name appears **over 1,000 times** in the DOJ files. The newly released documents reveal he was listed among the FBI's **"10 co-conspirators"** in August 2019 emails, identified as "a wealthy businessman in Ohio." His 1991 power of attorney granted Epstein "full power and authority to do and perform every act necessary" over finances, investments, and property. The January 30, 2026 DOJ release includes a **1998 purchase/sale agreement, a $10 million promissory note, and a personal guaranty** signed by Epstein detailing the Manhattan townhouse transfer mechanics (9 East 71st Street, which Wexner bought for $13.2 million in 1989). A 2008 investigative memo shows Wexner's attorneys told investigators Epstein had repaid $100 million — "thought to be just a portion of what he stole." House Oversight Democrats stated the total transferred was approximately **$1 billion**.

Wexner was deposed on February 18, 2026, in a five-hour session at his New Albany, Ohio, mansion. He called Epstein "a con man," denied friendship despite a birthday card signed "your friend Leslie," and said he visited the island "about an hour" with family, calling it "a pretty crummy island." His lawyer was caught on camera whispering answers and separately joked, "I'll f***ing kill you if you answer another question." The deposition video was released by the House Oversight Committee on February 19, 2026. In the Giuffre v. Maxwell filings, Maxwell denied providing Giuffre "an outfit of a sexual nature to wear for Les Wexner."

### Bill Gates: the post-conviction relationship

DOJ files document multiple Gates-Epstein meetings after the 2008 conviction: dinner in December 2010, meetings in April and October 2011, a Norway meeting in August 2012, and a possible February 2013 lunch. ## Court records and legal proceedings not commonly compiled

### Giuffre v. Maxwell: eight batches unsealed in January 2024

Judge Loretta Preska ordered eight batches released between January 3–9, 2024, revealing approximately **150 previously redacted names**. Key depositions include Ghislaine Maxwell's confidential April 22, 2016 videotaped deposition, Virginia Giuffre's detailed allegations, Johanna Sjoberg's testimony about Prince Andrew, **Juan Alessi's** deposition (Dkt. 1158) describing Maxwell taking topless photos of girls, and Alfredo Rodriguez's testimony. A **July 23, 2025 Second Circuit ruling** vacated parts of Judge Preska's orders and remanded for additional unsealing review, potentially yielding further document releases. The full docket is available at courtlistener.com/docket/4355835/giuffre-v-maxwell/.

### The Non-Prosecution Agreement: architecture of immunity

The NPA, signed September 24, 2007 — one day before the prosecutor was prepared to indict — granted immunity not only to Epstein but to "any potential co-conspirators," including four named individuals (Kellen, Ross, Groff, Marcinkova). The original **53-page draft federal indictment** with **60 criminal counts** prepared in May 2007 by AUSA Marie Villafaña was never filed. The full NPA text is available on DocumentCloud (documentcloud.org/documents/6184602-Jeffrey-Epstein-non-prosecution-agreement/). The DOJ Office of Professional Responsibility report (justice.gov/opr/page/file/1336471/dl) found Acosta's handling was "poor judgment" but not "professional misconduct."

### Crime Victims' Rights Act: "a tale of national disgrace"

Judge Kenneth Marra's **February 2019 ruling** found federal prosecutors violated the CVRA by failing to notify victims and "affirmatively misled" them. The Eleventh Circuit en banc decision (April 2021, 7-4) denied relief, finding no private right of action outside a preexisting criminal proceeding, but called the situation **"a tale of national disgrace."** The Supreme Court denied certiorari in 2022. The full en banc opinion is at media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/201913843.enb.pdf.

### Maxwell trial exhibits and evidence

Government Exhibit **GX 52** was the "little black book." Government Exhibit **GX 606** was the household manual instructing staff to "see nothing, hear nothing, say nothing" and "NEVER disclose Mr. Epstein or Ms. Maxwell's activities." Flight records (**GX 662**) showed Maxwell and Kellen flew together approximately 39 times in 2003. The FBI's **three-page evidence index** catalogs 40 computers/electronic devices, 26 storage drives, 70+ CDs, 6 recording devices, and **300+ GB of data** from seized devices. Three discs containing wiretap results from Maxwell's phone number have not been publicly released. The Maxwell grand jury transcripts were ultimately ordered released on December 9, 2025, under the Epstein Files Transparency Act (opinion at nysd.uscourts.gov).

## Financial trails: $1.5 billion in suspicious transactions

### Southern Trust Company and the USVI tax fraud

Southern Trust grew from $198.5 million in assets (2013) to $391.3 million (2017) while employing only 13 people (11 in administrative roles). The USVI AG alleged it "performed no informatics or data-mining services" and instead "acted as a conduit for payment to foreign women, credit cards, airplanes and other instrumentalities" of trafficking. Epstein personally received a **$71.3 million income tax exemption** through the entity's S-Corp election. A newly released document dated October 5, 2015 shows Epstein, through Southern Trust, signed a **$25 million contract with the Rothschild Group** for risk analysis services. Howard Lutnick and Epstein co-invested through **"Adfin"** — Epstein signed for Southern Trust, Lutnick for CVAFH I, per a 2012 contract in the DOJ release.

### Wire transfers: the $1.1 billion JPMorgan SAR

Senator Wyden's investigation uncovered **4,725 wire transfers** totaling nearly $1.1 billion flowing through just one Epstein JPMorgan account. Multiple banks filed massive retroactive suspicious activity reports after Epstein's 2019 arrest: JPMorgan ($1.1 billion, 4,700 transactions back to 2003), Deutsche Bank (~$400 million), **Bank of New York Mellon** ($378 million across 270 wire transfers, including 18 round-dollar $1 million wires in 2007 alone), and Bank of America (amount unspecified). Total flagged suspicious activity exceeds **$1.5 billion**. A previously unreported **DEA investigation** ("Operation Chain Reaction," 69-page law enforcement sensitive memo from 2015) examined Epstein for "$5.6 million in suspected narcotics-related" wire transfers in USVI and NYC.

### Gratitude America: phantom donations

NBC News contacted 56 listed grant recipients (2010–2017) and **10 denied receiving any donation** — including the Metropolitan Museum of Art, Duke University, Ohio State, and the Elton John AIDS Foundation. The foundation received most of its funding from a $10 million grant controlled by Leon Black. IRS filings are available on ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer (projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/660789697). A DOJ document (**EFTA00009962.pdf**) contains a WSJ journalist's email to SDNY noting prosecutors were scrutinizing Gratitude America because "the transactions took place within the past five years."

### Harvard: $9.1 million and key card access through 2018

Epstein donated $6.5 million in 2003 to establish the Program for Evolutionary Dynamics under Martin Nowak, plus additional gifts totaling $9.1 million (1998–2008). Despite a 2008 ban on Epstein gifts, Nowak gave him unrestricted key card access to Harvard offices as late as 2018. Epstein introduced donors who gave $7.5 million to Nowak's work and $2 million to George Church's genomics lab. Newly released DOJ emails show Epstein established an investment company (**Georgarage LLC**) with Church and separately funded **Androcyte**, later renamed BetterHumans, described as "the world's first specifically-transhumanist bio-medical research organization." Epstein's will left Nowak $5 million.

## Community research tools and key reference databases

The citizen research infrastructure has become remarkably sophisticated. **JMail** (jmail.world) provides a Gmail-clone interface for browsing Epstein's emails (jeevacation@gmail.com and jeeproject@yahoo.com) with sub-tools for photos, flights, files, and Amazon purchases — cataloging 1,038,603 emails as of February 19, 2026. The **GitHub repository rhowardstone/Epstein-research** contains forensic analysis of the full 194.5 GB release, including full-text indexing of every page, 2,587,102 redaction records, 1,530 audio/video transcripts, and a 1,536-person entity registry with 2,096 mapped connections. **Tommy Carstensen's video gallery** (tommycarstensen.com/epstein/video_gallery.html) indexes all DOJ video releases with AI transcriptions. The **Democracy Defenders Fund** filed an OIG complaint and discovered the DOJ "quietly altered previously released documents" — comparison on January 15, 2026 identified modified documents with additional redactions.

An NPR investigation published February 24, 2026 found **53 pages of interview documents and notes missing** from the public database relating to a Trump accuser interviewed by the FBI four times. Separately, community researchers identified approximately 23 documents they believe were never uploaded to the DOJ website (EFTA00709804–EFTA01135708 range), and specific EFTA documents that were temporarily removed, including **EFTA00000468** (Trump/Epstein/Maxwell photo) and **EFTA01660679** (a DOJ-compiled list of tips). The DOJ's "PEP list" of 305 Politically Exposed Persons was criticized as "obfuscation by overproduction" — it includes long-deceased individuals like Elvis Presley and Kurt Cobain.

## Conclusion: what remains hidden and what comes next

The released files reveal a financial operation of staggering scale — over **$1.5 billion** in flagged suspicious transactions — and a network far broader than any single document compilation can capture. Yet the most consequential finding may be structural: only half of the 6 million identified pages have been released, Dataset 9 remains incomplete at source, and the DOJ has acknowledged withholding entire categories of material. The three-page FBI evidence index describes **40 seized computers, 300+ GB of data, and wiretap discs** from Maxwell's phone that remain unreleased. Deputy AG Blanche stated on February 2, 2026 that there would be "no additional prosecutions," yet three international arrests followed within three weeks.

The most significant gaps for researchers are the Little St. James logbook and boat trip logs (referenced but not fully released), the complete contents of 26 storage drives and 70+ CDs seized from Epstein's properties, and the approximately 3 million pages the DOJ identified but did not release. The Clinton depositions scheduled for February 26–27 and the ongoing Senate Finance Committee battle for Treasury records from Secretary Bessent represent the most immediate potential sources of new information. For researchers building on existing compilations, Dataset 11's financial ledgers, Dataset 9's email evidence (despite its incompleteness), and the EFTA document numbers flagged by the rhowardstone GitHub repository represent the highest-value targets for systematic review.
