Section 1

Overview: The Epstein Files Disclosure

On January 30, 2026, the United States Department of Justice executed one of the most significant disclosures of investigative material in U.S. history, releasing approximately 3.5 million pages of documents, 2,000+ videos, and 180,000 images related to Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell's sex trafficking network. This release was mandated by the Epstein Files Transparency Act, signed into law by President Trump on November 19, 2025.

The disclosure eclipsed all previous releases in scope. While prior disclosures were targeted (unsealing specific case records), this was a comprehensive data dump driven by a statutory deadline and the DOJ's "collect everything" strategy, which gathered over 6 million pages from across the federal apparatus before filtering to the 3.5 million deemed responsive.

Scale of the archive: As of February 19, 2026, the community platform Jmail has indexed 1,412,250 files, 2,474,242 pages, and 1,038,603 emails from the DOJ release plus 8,624 documents from the House Oversight Committee.

Archive Architecture & Data Set Breakdown

Information synthesized from the Eren Özkaradeniz technical guide (Medium) and DOJ Epstein Library.

The DOJ repository at justice.gov/epstein is the sole legally authorized source. It uses HTTPS on a .gov domain, enforces an age-verification gate (due to graphic content), and is organized into four "pillars" representing different legal/investigative sources.

Redaction policy: The DOJ applied blanket visual redactions — every female depicted was treated as a potential victim (blurred/blacked out), except Ghislaine Maxwell. Males are unredacted unless linked to a protected female subject. This has drawn criticism for both over- and under-redaction: some victim names and nude photos were accidentally left unredacted, causing temporary takedowns, while other redactions may shield perpetrators.

Repository Structure (Four Pillars)

Pillar I: DOJ Disclosures

The 3.5 million pages from the Jan 30, 2026 release. Data Sets 1–12. The primary focus of current research.

Pillar II: Court Records

Filings from specific cases (e.g., Doe v. Indyke, Maxwell trial). Includes Epstein's will and testament.

Pillar III: House Oversight

8,624 documents released by the House Oversight Committee. Estate management records and supplementary exhibits.

Pillar IV: FBI Vault / FOIA

Pre-existing FOIA releases. Heavily redacted but contain investigative records and internal agency correspondence.

Section 3

Research Tools & Navigation Resources

Synthesized from the Resource Guide and technical guide sources.

The raw DOJ repository, while authoritative, presents significant usability challenges: no master file index, no file-type browsing, disabled automated crawling, and imperfect OCR on scanned/handwritten documents. Several community and media tools have emerged to address this.

Primary DOJ Epstein Library

justice.gov/epstein — The sole legally authorized source. Always use as baseline reference. Has a built-in search bar ("Search Full Epstein Library") but warns that handwritten pages, images, and some scans may not be electronically searchable.

Search Jmail.World

Community-built Gmail-style interface for Epstein emails. 1M+ emails indexed. Includes JeffTube (YouTube-style video browser) and Jwiki (Wikipedia-style individual profiles). Launched Feb 2026; 1.3M+ views within hours.

Search Courier / Google Pinpoint

Courier Newsroom uploaded 20,000+ estate documents to Google Pinpoint, enabling OCR searching across mixed PDF folders. Retained items deleted by DOJ. Separate databases for DOJ releases and estate files.

Search CBS News Database

Searchable interface allowing filtering by Data Set and document type. Professional editorial team examining files and reporting findings.

Technical GitHub Repositories

paulgp/epstein-document-search — Python scripts to download, split, and index PDFs into a local Meilisearch instance. yung-megafone/Epstein-Files — Archive index with torrent magnets, SHA256 hashes, and integrity verification for all 12 data sets.

OSINT Epstein OSINT Database (Notion)

Living, open-source archive with structured categories for individuals, organizations, flight logs, properties, and docket entries. Useful for network mapping and entity tracking.

Recommended Research Workflow

Based on guidance across all sources, the optimal two-step process is: (1) Discovery via indexed/searchable tools (Jmail, Pinpoint, CBS), then (2) Verification against original DOJ PDFs. OCR text may be imperfect; metadata may be mislabeled; user annotations require rigorous checking. Always cross-reference findings across multiple repositories.

Katie Johnson Testimony — Document Analysis

Content warning: This section summarizes testimony containing graphic descriptions of sexual assault against a minor. The uploaded document is a transcription of a video interview dated February 11, 2016, transcribed by TurboScribe.ai.

The uploaded document is a full transcription of testimony by "Katie Johnson" (a pseudonym used in legal filings), who states she came to the interview voluntarily and under oath. The testimony describes events she alleges occurred when she was 13 years old, involving both Jeffrey Epstein and Donald Trump at Epstein's Manhattan residence.

Key Claims in the Testimony

Recruitment: Johnson states she was recruited by a woman called "Tiffany" at a bus station while trying to become a model. Tiffany promised modeling connections and brought her to parties hosted by Epstein. Johnson describes the other girls present as being approximately 13–16 years old.

Epstein's role: Johnson states that Epstein conducted a "private interview" at the first party, asked her to undress to her undergarments for what she believed was a figure assessment, asked her age (she said 13), and then attempted penetration, which she resisted. She states he forced penetration at a later party during a massage session.

Trump encounters — four incidents described:

  • First encounter: At an Epstein party, Trump asked for a hand job. Johnson describes being told "no one touches Mr. Trump's penis without a glove." She complied. Trump left immediately after.
  • Second encounter: Trump requested oral sex through Tiffany. Johnson describes a condom requirement and being scolded by Tiffany for approaching without one.
  • Third encounter: A "maid fantasy" role-play involving Johnson and a Hispanic girl named "Maria." Johnson describes Trump using racial slurs, threatening to call immigration on Maria, and forcing both girls into sexual acts.
  • Fourth encounter (final): Described as a "rape fantasy" in which Johnson was tied to a bed. She states it escalated beyond any agreed scenario — she screamed, Tiffany attempted to intervene, and Trump forcibly took her virginity while she cried and begged him to stop. Afterward, she says Trump told her she "should be glad that someone like Donald Trump popped my cherry, and not some pimply little 14-year-old." When she asked about pregnancy, he allegedly said "you'll get an abortion then, bitch" and left. Johnson states she never returned.

Contextual Details from the Testimony

  • Johnson states Trump specifically requested she wear a blonde wig because she "reminded him of his daughter," and Tiffany confirmed to Trump that Johnson was the same age as his daughter.
  • Johnson describes Trump making racist comments about Jewish, Hispanic, Arabic, and Black people in conversations with Epstein before parties.
  • Johnson states that after parties, girls were required to report to Epstein about everything that happened, including client preferences and requests, before being paid.
  • Johnson describes lasting psychological damage: inability to maintain healthy relationships, self-sabotage, destroyed self-esteem, and recent entry into therapy.
  • She states she told only one friend (since eighth grade) and destroyed all evidence to prevent her father from finding out.
Important context: This testimony was recorded on February 11, 2016, during the presidential campaign. A federal lawsuit (Doe v. Trump, filed in 2016) made similar allegations but was voluntarily withdrawn before trial. The plaintiff stated she received death threats. The claims have not been adjudicated in court. The testimony should be assessed alongside the DOJ file evidence (FBI reports, victim statements, court transcripts) documented in other sections of this briefing.

Corroborating Threads in DOJ Files

Several elements of the Johnson testimony align with patterns documented in the DOJ releases:

  • The FBI report (Data Set 9, EFTA00095502) independently describes a witness who "saw Epstein and Trump together a lot" and that "Trump was always trying to impress Epstein."
  • The Mar-a-Lago abuse thread documents underage models at parties where Trump and Epstein socialized (Capponi testimony, Jane Doe 3).

  • The Fifth Amendment exchange in court establishes that Epstein invoked the Fifth on questions about socializing with Trump and underage children.

  • The "reporting to Epstein after parties" pattern Johnson describes is consistent with how Epstein's operation is documented elsewhere in the files — a surveillance and intelligence-gathering operation tied to his social network.

Best Practices Applied

  • Primary source priority: All document references trace to DOJ file identifiers (EFTA numbers) and direct justice.gov URLs.
  • Cross-referencing: Claims checked across multiple data sets and sources where possible.
  • Contextual framing: Allegations are noted as allegations; testimony is identified as unajudicated; official narratives are distinguished from contradicting evidence.
  • Redaction awareness: The DOJ's redaction policy (blanket female redaction, encoding artifacts from PDF conversion) is noted as a factor affecting research completeness.
  • Tool limitations acknowledged: AI-assisted searches and community platforms are augmentative, not definitive. OCR imperfections in scanned documents may produce unreliable keyword results.
For continued research: Start at justice.gov/epstein for authoritative files. Use Jmail.world for email search, Google Pinpoint for OCR document search, and GitHub repositories for bulk download and local indexing. Always verify community findings against DOJ originals.
