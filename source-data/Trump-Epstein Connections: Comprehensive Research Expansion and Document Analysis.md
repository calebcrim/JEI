# Trump-Epstein connections: comprehensive research expansion

**The most significant new developments since 2024 center on massive DOJ document releases totaling 3.5 million pages, Congressional investigations, and investigative journalism revealing that Trump is mentioned over 1,000 times in the Epstein files.** Multiple previously unknown victim allegations, flight records contradicting Trump's public denials, and evidence that the DOJ withheld or removed Trump-related documents have emerged. Below is a full accounting organized by priority.

## Part 1: New information not likely in existing notes

### DOJ file releases reveal Trump flew on Epstein's jet far more than known

A January 2020 email from an SDNY prosecutor, released in the December 22–23, 2025 DOJ batch (~30,000 pages), stated: "For your situational awareness, we wanted to let you know that the flight records we received yesterday reflect that Donald Trump traveled on Epstein's private jet many more times than we previously had reported or that we were aware of." Federal records now show Trump as a passenger on **at least 8 flights between 1993 and 1996**. On at least four, Ghislaine Maxwell was present. On one 1993 flight, only Trump and Epstein were listed. Pilot Larry Visoski confirmed Trump as a passenger during Maxwell trial testimony in December 2021. Specific documented flights include: May 15, 1994 (Palm Beach to Teterboro via D.C., with Marla Maples and infant Tiffany Trump); August 13, 1995 (Palm Beach to Teterboro, with Eric Trump and Maxwell); January 5, 1997 (Palm Beach to Newark). This directly contradicted Trump's 2024 statement: "I was never on Epstein's Plane." All documented flights were domestic routes — no evidence places Trump on flights to Little St. James.

### Epstein's "knew about the girls" email and other explosive 2011–2019 correspondence

On November 12, 2025, House Oversight Committee Democrats released three pivotal emails from the Epstein files:

**Email 1 (April 2, 2011, Epstein to Maxwell):** "I want you to realize that that dog that hasn't barked is trump.. [VICTIM] spent hours at my house with him,, he has never once been mentioned. police chief. etc. im 75% there." Maxwell replied: "I have been thinking about that." House Republicans identified the redacted victim as Virginia Giuffre. This contradicts Giuffre's sworn deposition testimony that she never saw Trump at Epstein's homes.

**Email 2 (January 31, 2019, Epstein to Michael Wolff):** "[Victim] mara lago. [identifier]. Trump said he asked me to resign, never a member ever. Of course he knew about the girls as he asked ghislaine to stop." This is the most widely covered email in the releases, generating massive press attention.

**Email 3 (December 2015, Epstein-Wolff exchange):** Wolff advised Epstein on how to exploit Trump's potential denials about their relationship for "valuable PR and political currency."

Additional emails released the same day by House Republicans (from 23,000 pages) included a **2018 Epstein email** stating "I am the one able to take him down" (referring to Trump) and a **2017 email to Larry Summers**: "I have met some very bad people, none as bad as trump. Not one decent cell in his body."

### 14-year-old introduced to Trump at Mar-a-Lago (1994) — the "Jane" / Interlochen victim

A court document in the December 2025 DOJ release described a girl taken to Mar-a-Lago in 1994 when she was approximately 14 years old. She was recruited by Epstein at Interlochen Center for the Arts summer camp in Michigan. In her FBI 302 interview, she stated Epstein introduced her to Trump, and Epstein "elbowed Trump playfully, asking him, 'This is a good one, right?'" Trump "smiled and nodded." She testified at Maxwell's 2021 trial under the pseudonym "Jane." She described meeting Trump at Mar-a-Lago but **did not describe inappropriate behavior by Trump** during testimony. Per NPR's February 24, 2026 investigation, her FBI interview was temporarily removed from the DOJ's public database; an interview with her mother — who recalled hearing "a prince and DONALD TRUMP visited EPSTEIN's house" — remains offline.

### Previously unknown accuser alleges assault at age ~13 (circa 1983)

An FBI memo from the January 30, 2026 release detailed a woman's claim that around 1983, when she was approximately 13, Epstein introduced her to Trump, "who subsequently forced her head down to his exposed penis which she subsequently bit. In response, Trump punched her in the head and kicked her out." The FBI interviewed this accuser **four times**. Per NPR's February 24, 2026 investigation, only the first interview (July 24, 2019) is in the public database, and it does not mention Trump. Of **15 documents** listed in a Maxwell case discovery log for this accuser, only 7 are publicly available. **53 pages of interview documents appear to be missing.** Her attorney said she "was concerned about implicating additional individuals, and specifically any that were well known, due to fear of retaliation." FBI agents noted she identified Epstein from "a widely distributed photograph" of Epstein with Trump.

### NPR investigation: DOJ withheld or removed 50+ pages of Trump-related files

Published February 24, 2026, by Stephen Fowler, this is the most significant breaking development. NPR's investigation found more than 50 pages of FBI interviews and notes from conversations with women who accused Trump of sexual abuse as minors have been withheld from the public database despite the Epstein Files Transparency Act mandating their release. NPR identified the gaps by reviewing unique serial numbers stamped onto documents and comparing them against FBI case records, emails, and discovery logs. Sixteen files disappeared from the DOJ site after initial posting, including a Trump photo. AG Bondi and Deputy AG Blanche wrote to Congress on February 14, 2026, insisting no records were withheld "on the basis of embarrassment, reputational harm, or political sensitivity."

### Wall Street Journal birthday letter story and Trump's $10 billion lawsuit

On July 17, 2025, the Wall Street Journal (reporters Khadeeja Safdar and Joe Palazzolo) reported on a birthday book compiled by Maxwell for Epstein's 50th birthday in 2003. A letter bearing Trump's name contained "several lines of typewritten text framed by the outline of a naked woman" with Trump's signature "mimicking pubic hair." The letter concluded: **"Happy Birthday — and may every day be another wonderful secret."** Trump called it "a FAKE" and on July 18 filed a **$10 billion defamation lawsuit** against Dow Jones, News Corp, Rupert Murdoch, and both reporters. WSJ filed a motion to dismiss in September 2025, arguing the article is true — Epstein's estate produced the birthday book matching the description. The House Oversight Committee released the actual letter in September 2025, confirming WSJ's reporting. As of late 2025, the case remained pending.

### Virginia Giuffre died by suicide in April 2025

Virginia Giuffre, the most prominent Epstein accuser and the woman recruited from Mar-a-Lago's spa, died by suicide on April 25, 2025, at age 41, at her farm in Western Australia. She had been in custody and divorce proceedings. Her death removed the possibility of further testimony. Her posthumous memoir "Nobody's Girl" (published October 21, 2025) described meeting Trump at Mar-a-Lago: "Trump couldn't have been friendlier, telling me it was fantastic that I was there. 'Do you like kids?' he asked" — reportedly offering to help her find babysitting jobs. **The memoir contains no allegations of wrongdoing by Trump.** In July 2025, Trump told reporters Epstein "stole" Giuffre from his Mar-a-Lago spa. Giuffre's family expressed shock, saying she "wasn't stolen, she was preyed upon at his property."

### Maxwell "presented" a victim to Trump at a New York party

An FBI 302 from mid-2021 (released January 30, 2026) described an Epstein victim who told investigators that Maxwell once "presented" her to Trump at a party in New York. Maxwell said: "Oh I think he likes you. Aren't you lucky. This is great." The FBI memo noted the encounter was "set up very much like how Maxwell introduced her to Epstein." The woman told federal investigators **"nothing happened"** between her and Trump.

### Stacey Williams — new public accuser (October 2024)

Former Sports Illustrated swimsuit model Stacey Williams alleged in October 2024 (on a "Survivors for Kamala" Zoom call) that Trump groped her when Epstein brought her to Trump Tower in the early 1990s. She stated: "I felt so humiliated and so sick to my stomach… I was rolled in there like a piece of meat." She said Epstein talked about Trump "every time we spoke" and that Trump sent her a handwritten postcard featuring Mar-a-Lago: "Stacey — Your home away from home. Love Donald."

### Epstein Files Transparency Act and Trump's attempts to block it

The House voted **427–1** on November 18, 2025 (Rep. Clay Higgins sole dissent); the Senate passed unanimously the same day. Trump signed it into law November 19, without reporters present. The Act required DOJ to release all files within 30 days. Reporting indicates Trump **personally lobbied individual GOP members to oppose the bill** but was "outmaneuvered by a bipartisan groundswell." The DOJ ultimately released approximately **3.5 million pages, 2,000 videos, and 180,000 images** across multiple batches (December 19, 2025 through January 30, 2026). Trump is mentioned **more than 1,000 times** across the full corpus.

### Faulty redactions reveal unverified FBI tip about infant killing

In the December 19, 2025 initial release, social media users discovered blacked-out text could be revealed by copy-pasting. Among recovered content: an unverified FBI tip alleging Trump had witnessed the killing and disposal of an infant born to a 13-year-old trafficking victim. This was explicitly described as an **unverified tip** from the FBI's National Threat Operations Center. The DOJ stated the files contained "untrue and sensationalist claims made against President Trump that were submitted to the FBI right before the 2020 election."

### FBI compiled internal list of Trump assault allegations (August 2025)

An internal FBI list from the National Threat Operations Center compiled "more than a dozen allegations related to Trump." Officials sorted them with "Yellow highlighting is for the salacious piece." Most were marked as unverifiable or not credible. One lead — the ~1983 accuser described above — was forwarded to the FBI's Washington Office to arrange a formal interview. Additional NTOC tips included allegations of Trump being a "client for sex-trafficking" at Trump Golf Course in Rancho Palos Verdes, California (1995–1996) with Maxwell as "broker," and separate claims of "big orgy parties" with Trump and Clinton at Epstein's New York residence.

### O'Keefe secret recording of DOJ official (September 2025)

James O'Keefe published a secret recording on September 4, 2025, with DOJ acting Deputy Chief Joseph Schnitt, who stated: "They'll redact every Republican or conservative person in those files, leave all the liberal, Democratic people." Schnitt also said Maxwell's transfer to a minimum-security prison camp was "against BOP policy because she's a convicted sex offender" and that Bondi "wants whatever Trump wants." DOJ said Schnitt had "no role in the Department's internal review of Epstein materials."

### Limousine driver report (1995, released 2025)

A document from October 2020 (released in the December 2025 batch) referenced a 1995 limousine ride where Trump allegedly referenced "Epstein" and "abusing some girl." The driver also reported a woman claiming "Donald J. Trump had raped her along with Jeffrey Epstein." The woman was later "found with her head 'blown off' in Kiefer, OK." FBI agents found a photo of Trump with Maxwell while examining Steve Bannon's iPhone 7 using Cellebrite forensic tools in June 2021. This was documented in the December 2025 release.

### Mar-a-Lago subpoenaed in Maxwell case (2021)

Trump's Mar-a-Lago club was subpoenaed in 2021 to produce employment records relating to a redacted individual in the Maxwell case. This was revealed in the December 2025 DOJ release.

### Trump sued NYT for $15 billion (September 2025)

On September 15, 2025, Trump filed a **$15 billion lawsuit** against the New York Times, Penguin Random House, and four reporters, citing reputational damage from Epstein coverage. Judge Merryday dismissed the initial complaint as "tediously and burdensomely" improper on September 19. Trump refiled within 28 days.

### Maxwell's clemency offer (February 2026)

At her February 10, 2026 House Oversight Committee deposition, Maxwell invoked the Fifth Amendment and refused to answer questions. Her lawyer offered testimony exonerating both Trump and Clinton in exchange for clemency: "Both President Trump and President Clinton are innocent of any wrongdoing. Ms. Maxwell alone can explain why."

### Trump administration officials named in Epstein files

Commerce Secretary **Howard Lutnick**, Navy Secretary **John Phelan**, and Deputy Defense Secretary **Steve Feinberg** all have names or correspondence in released files. Lutnick denied maintaining contact with Epstein past 2005, but records show continued relationship after Epstein's 2008 guilty plea.

## Part 3: Key court documents and official records

### The "Katie Johnson" / Jane Doe lawsuit — complete history

**First filing (April 26, 2016):** Filed pro se in U.S. District Court, Central District of California (Riverside), as *Katie Johnson v. Donald J. Trump et al.*, Case No. 5:16-cv-00797. Sought $100 million. Dismissed May 2, 2016 — filing address was abandoned, phone unverifiable.

**Second filing (June 2016):** Refiled in New York federal court as "Jane Doe."

**Third filing (September 2016):** Refiled in SDNY with attorneys Thomas Meagher, Evan Goldman, and Cheney Mason. Included "Tiffany Doe" affidavit (a woman who claimed to have recruited girls for Epstein's parties) and "Joan Doe" (alleged classmate). Key allegation: "I loudly pleaded with Defendant Trump to stop, but he did not. Defendant Trump responded to my pleas by violently striking me in the face." Tiffany Doe's affidavit: "I personally witnessed Defendant Trump telling the Plaintiff that she shouldn't ever say anything if she didn't want to disappear like the 12-year-old female Maria."

**Withdrawn November 4, 2016** — six days before the election. Lisa Bloom stated the plaintiff received "a number of threats" and was "too afraid" to appear at a November 2 press conference. Attorney Goldman said "Johnson's car and cellphone were stolen."

**Credibility concerns:** The Guardian traced promotion of the case to "Al Taylor," later identified as **Norm Lubow**, former "Jerry Springer Show" producer. Julie K. Brown (Miami Herald) said: "The woman just wouldn't talk, and her lawyer would not confirm she was even legit." However, attorney Cheney Mason told journalist Tara Palmeri in 2025 that he "still insists she told the truth." Michael Cohen confirmed he was instructed by Trump to "take care of it," sending a private investigator to track the accuser.

**FBI files (January 30, 2026):** An FBI complaint form details the allegations from this woman, consistent with the lawsuit. An NTOC report stated: "Complainant reported Donald Trump participated regularly in paying money to force her to perform sex acts with him."

### Virginia Giuffre's sworn testimony on Trump

In her November 2016 deposition in *Giuffre v. Maxwell* (S.D.N.Y., No. 15-cv-07433):
- "Have you ever been in Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein's presence with one another?" — **"No."**
- "Did you ever see Donald Trump at Jeffrey's home?" — **"Not that I can remember."**
- On the claim Trump flirted with her: "It's true that he didn't partake in any sex with us, and — but **it's not true that he flirted with me. Donald Trump never flirted with me.**"
- "I don't think Donald Trump participated in anything… I never saw or witnessed Donald Trump participate in those acts."

### Brad Edwards' affidavit and Trump's 2009 cooperation

Attorney Brad Edwards (representing Epstein victims) stated Trump was **"the only person"** among Epstein's associates who cooperated. Trump's attorney arranged a phone conference in lieu of deposition. Edwards said Trump was "very helpful" and "gave no indication whatsoever that he was involved in anything untoward." Trump told Edwards he'd "been to Jeffrey Epstein's house and saw young girls. Not underage girls." However, a 2009 email summarizing the call (reviewed by Rep. Jamie Raskin in 2025) quoted Trump as saying Epstein "was not a member of his club at Mar-a-Lago, but he was a guest, and he had never been asked to leave" — contradicting later claims of banning Epstein.

Trump's entry contained **16 phone numbers** (one of the highest counts), including emergency, car, and numbers for Trump's security guard and houseman. His was among **five names circled or highlighted**. Listed family members: Ivana Trump, Ivanka Trump, Robert Trump, Blaine Trump, and Melania. The 97-page book was taken by former butler Alfredo Rodriguez, who tried to sell it and later provided a copy to the FBI. Gawker published a redacted version in 2015. AG Bondi released it again in February 2025.

No documented evidence places Trump at Epstein's 7,600-acre Zorro Ranch near Stanley, New Mexico. Multiple survivors testified about abuse there (Annie Farmer, "Jane," Giuffre), but none placed Trump at the property. New Mexico AG Raúl Torrez reopened a criminal investigation into the ranch as of February 2026. The ranch was purchased in 2023 by Don Huffines, a self-described "Trump Republican."

## Critical assessment and flags

**Most significant new findings not in existing notes:**
1. The NPR investigation documenting 50+ withheld pages of FBI interviews with Trump accusers (Feb. 24, 2026)
2. Epstein's "knew about the girls" email and the "dog that hasn't barked" email about Trump and Giuffre
3. Federal flight records showing 8+ flights contradicting Trump's "never on Epstein's Plane" claim
4. The ~1983 accuser's FBI interviews (four total, most withheld)
5. The "Jane"/Interlochen victim's account of Epstein introducing her to Trump at Mar-a-Lago at age 14
6. Maxwell's clemency offer to exonerate Trump in exchange for release
7. Trump's 2006 call to police chief: "everyone has known he's been doing this" — contradicting his 2019 "no idea" claim
8. Bannon-Epstein communications totaling hundreds of messages
9. The birthday letter and Trump's $10B/$15B lawsuits against WSJ and NYT
10. Trump personally lobbying to block the Epstein Files Transparency Act

**Reliability spectrum:** The allegations range from sworn depositions and trial testimony (highest reliability), through FBI 302 interview memos (moderate — investigator summaries), to anonymous NTOC tips (lowest — many flagged as "not credible" or "false" by agents). Epstein's own emails are self-serving statements by a convicted sex offender. The DOJ's dual role — simultaneously releasing damaging files while labeling them false — creates an inherently conflicted framework. Virginia Giuffre's consistent sworn testimony that Trump did not engage in wrongdoing remains the strongest exculpatory evidence, though her death forecloses further testimony, and Epstein's private emails claim she "spent hours at my house with him."
