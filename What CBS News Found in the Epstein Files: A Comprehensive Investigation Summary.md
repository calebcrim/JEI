# What CBS News found in the Epstein files

The CBS News Epstein Files investigation — powered by a searchable database of DOJ releases hosted on Google Journalist Studio's Pinpoint platform — represents one of the most sustained newsroom efforts to examine the estimated **6 million pages** of documents released under the Epstein Files Transparency Act. Across 12 Data Sets spanning FBI interviews, prison surveillance, emails, financial records, flight manifests, and **180,000 images plus 2,000 videos** seized from Epstein's properties, CBS reporters have uncovered previously unknown federal investigations, identified critical anomalies in the death inquiry, documented financial networks reaching into the current presidential administration, and corroborated victim testimony describing a systematic trafficking operation. What follows synthesizes every major finding CBS News has reported across the four areas of inquiry.

## Trump appeared in the files more than 38,000 times

CBS News reported **"hundreds of references to Mr. Trump"** in the DOJ document tranche, while a New York Times proprietary analysis — widely cited by CBS and other outlets — identified more than **5,300 files containing over 38,000 references** to Trump, Melania Trump, Mar-a-Lago, and related terms. Many references appear in news clippings shared among DOJ employees, but a significant number come from original investigative documents, witness interviews, and Epstein's own correspondence.

**Flight logs revealed deeper ties than publicly known.** A 2020 internal email from an assistant U.S. attorney in the Southern District of New York, released in Data Set 8, stated: *"Donald Trump traveled on Epstein's private jet many more times than previously has been reported (or that we were aware)."* The prosecutor noted Trump took at least one flight with Epstein and a 20-year-old (name redacted), plus two flights with *"women who would be possible witnesses in a Maxwell case."* The email, flagged for "situational awareness," reflected analysis of more than 100 pages of flight records received the prior day.

**Epstein's own emails were more direct.** In an April 2, 2011 email to Ghislaine Maxwell found in Data Set 9, Epstein wrote: *"I want you to realize that that dog that hasn't barked is Trump. [Victim 1] spent hours at my house with him, he has never once been mentioned."* Maxwell replied: *"I have been thinking about that…"* In a January 31, 2019 email to journalist Michael Wolff, Epstein stated: *"Trump said he asked me to resign, never a member ever. Of course he knew about the girls as he asked ghislaine to stop."* A separate December 2015 exchange showed Wolff advising Epstein to let Trump "hang himself" if Trump denied visiting Epstein's home or plane, calling such denials *"valuable PR and political currency."*

**The Fifth Amendment deposition**, widely reported by other outlets but not deeply covered by CBS, recorded Epstein in 2010 invoking his Fifth, Sixth, and Fourteenth Amendment rights when asked: *"Have you ever socialized with Donald Trump in the presence of females under the age of 18?"* CBS focused more on Ghislaine Maxwell's February 2026 invocation of the Fifth before the House Oversight Committee, where her attorney stated *"both President Trump and President Clinton are innocent of any wrongdoing. Ms. Maxwell alone can explain why."*

**Melania Trump's email to Maxwell**, released in Data Set 9, dated October 23, 2002, read: *"Dear G! How are you? Nice story about JE in NY mag. You look great in the picture… Give me a call when you are back in NY."* Maxwell replied warmly, calling her "Sweet pea." The files also contained a Tatler magazine image of Maxwell, Donald Trump, and Melania Trump together. **Former Palm Beach Police Chief Michael Reiter told the FBI** in an October 2019 interview that Trump had called him during the original investigation, saying: *"Thank goodness you're stopping him, everyone has known he's been doing this."* Trump called Maxwell *"evil,"* said he once was around Epstein with teenagers present but *"got the hell out of there,"* and claimed he kicked Epstein out of Mar-a-Lago.

CBS also broke the story that **at least 15 files disappeared from the DOJ website** after the initial December 19, 2025 release — including one showing a photo of Trump, Epstein, and Maxwell in an open desk drawer alongside framed photos of Clinton and the Pope. The DOJ claimed temporary removal for "victim review" before reposting.

## The Barrack connection and the child rape lawsuit that circulated inside Epstein's network

CBS News published a major exclusive on February 14, 2026, analyzing **over 100 texts and email exchanges** between Epstein and **Tom Barrack**, Trump's longtime confidant and current U.S. Ambassador to Turkey. Barrack maintained regular contact with Epstein from at least 2009 through 2018 — years after Epstein's 2008 sex offender conviction. Epstein regarded Barrack as a *"potential conduit for passing information to Mr. Trump,"* and Barrack replied *"on the bosses list"* (referring to Trump) in one exchange. Epstein facilitated introductions between Barrack and Peter Thiel, Ehud Barak, and Russia's UN Ambassador Vitaly Churkin.

Most strikingly, in April 2016, **Epstein forwarded Barrack an email about the "Katie Johnson" civil lawsuit** — in which a woman using a pseudonym alleged Epstein and Trump raped her in 1994 — writing it was *"nuts but I thought you guys should know."* The lawsuit was withdrawn before the 2016 election. NPR later reported the DOJ withheld at least 50 pages of FBI interview materials related to a separate accuser who alleged Trump sexually assaulted her as a minor around 1983 after an introduction by Epstein. The DOJ labeled some of these allegations *"untrue and sensationalist claims submitted to the FBI right before the 2020 election."*

On Epstein's calendar for August 29, 2016, a lunch was listed with Barrack, Ehud Barak, and Vitaly Churkin. **The day after that lunch, Epstein purchased approximately $1 million in Colony Capital stock** — Barrack's publicly traded real estate company. After Trump's election victory, a senior Saudi official texted Epstein asking about protocol for King Salman to call Trump; Epstein replied: *"TOM barrack is your point person."* CBS found no evidence the communications were passed to Trump or that Barrack participated in criminal conduct.

## A $1.1 billion financial web the DOJ nearly ignored

CBS News's financial reporting on the Epstein files revealed an astonishing breadth of illicit money flows that multiple federal agencies investigated but never prosecuted. The network's most explosive exclusive came on February 23, 2026: a **previously undisclosed DEA investigation** spanning five-plus years (opened December 17, 2010) that targeted Epstein and 14 other individuals for suspicious wire transfers linked to narcotics and prostitution. The 69-page memo documented approximately **$50 million in suspicious wire transfers** and identified Epstein bank accounts in **Switzerland, France, the Cayman Islands, and New York**. Companies named included SLK Designs LLC and Hyperion Air, both controlled by Epstein's attorney Darren Indyke. Remarkably, SDNY prosecutors who later charged Epstein in 2019 told CBS they were **not aware** of the earlier DEA probe.

**Senator Ron Wyden** revealed to CBS News that Treasury Department records show **4,700 potentially suspicious wire transfers totaling nearly $1.1 billion** flowing through just one Epstein bank account, with hundreds of millions more through other accounts. Wyden cited **$1.5 billion** in total suspicious financial transactions flagged by banks. A 2007 draft prosecution memo addressed to then-U.S. Attorney Alex Acosta — released in the files — revealed that **money laundering was among charges "considered and rejected"** by federal prosecutors in the original case.

**Les Wexner's financial entanglement** received extensive CBS coverage. The Victoria's Secret billionaire hired Epstein as his money manager in the mid-1980s, granted him power of attorney in 1991, and ultimately had **$46 million misappropriated** by Epstein. His Manhattan townhouse — worth over $50 million — was transferred to Epstein. FBI documents initially redacted, then un-redacted, listed Wexner as a **"co-conspirator."** His name appeared more than **4,000 times** in the files. Wexner testified before the House Oversight Committee that he was *"duped by a world-class con man."*

CBS also exclusively reported that **Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick** co-signed business documents with Epstein for a company called Adfin in December 2012 — contradicting Lutnick's public claim of cutting ties in 2005. Epstein's assistant forwarded Lutnick documents about casino legislation in the U.S. Virgin Islands. Separately, CBS's exclusive analysis of **hundreds of American Express travel bookings** revealed Epstein used an Amex Black Card to arrange international flights for women and girls from **Russia, Poland, Belarus, Latvia, and Ukraine** between 2012 and 2019 — well after his conviction. American Express told CBS: *"We regret having him as a customer."*

The **Rybolovlev mansion deal** appeared in Epstein's emails to Wolff in Data Set 9. Epstein theorized that Russian oligarch Dmitry Rybolovlev's $95 million purchase of a Palm Beach mansion from Trump — more than double the purchase price, during the 2008 financial crisis — was effectively Rybolovlev *"buying the house from himself"* through Trump as a front. Epstein explicitly questioned Trump's tax reporting: *"He should have had a 50 million plus capital gain… Key question how did he report the sale."* CBS noted these were Epstein's speculations, not established facts.

## Nineteen Jane Does and a recruitment machine that spanned continents

The DOJ files contained a **32-count draft indictment** from the Southern District of Florida naming **19 victims labeled Jane Doe #1 through #19**, with the youngest described as 14 years old. Approximately 11 attended the same Palm Beach County school. A companion 45-page prosecution memo addressed to Alex Acosta described Epstein as *"an extremely high flight risk and a continued danger to the community"* and recommended a sealed indictment on May 15, 2007. The indictment was never approved; the notorious plea deal followed instead.

The Palm Beach Police Department had identified **27 girls and women between ages 14 and 23** who visited Epstein's home for "massage services." FBI grand jury testimony confirmed *"more than a majority would have been under the age of 18."* A December 2019 co-conspirator investigation memo contained statements from **24 women abused as minors and 14 abused as adults**, referencing 10 co-conspirators. The files documented a recruitment pyramid: victims were **paid $200 per girl they brought** to Epstein, and recruiter Haley Robson was told by Epstein *"the younger the better"* when she brought a 23-year-old.

**Virginia Giuffre**, who died by suicide in April 2025 at age 41, was recruited at 16 while working at Mar-a-Lago. Her co-author Amy Wallace told CBS that Giuffre *"didn't just believe"* the files contained other abusers' names — *"she knew it."* CBS covered Giuffre's family's reaction when Trump said Epstein had "stolen" workers from Mar-a-Lago's spa, including Giuffre. Her brother Skye Roberts said the DOJ was *"redacting the names of perpetrators and unredacting the names of victims."* Attorneys for survivors confirmed the DOJ **failed to redact the identities of at least 31 child victims**.

A criminal investigative file included a chart of **six women identified as recruiters or facilitators**, led by Ghislaine Maxwell. Named individuals included **Sarah Kellen** (listed as unindicted co-conspirator, described as greeting girls and leading them upstairs), **Lesley Groff** (executive assistant who arranged international travel), **Adriana Ross** (who allegedly removed computer drives before a police search), and **Nadia Marcinkova** (reportedly purchased by Epstein at age 15 from her family in Yugoslavia). CBS independently obtained a **1996 NYPD report** in which Maria Farmer — who reported Epstein to the FBI more than 20 years before he faced federal charges — described threats from Epstein. The 1996 complaint was categorized as a child pornography case and went uninvestigated for over a decade.

## Ten broken cameras and an orange figure on the stairs at 10:39 PM

CBS News's Investigative Unit, led by producer Daniel Ruetenik with reporters Graham Kates and Cara Tabachnick, produced the most granular reconstruction of Epstein's final hours of any newsroom. Data Set 8 contained approximately **400 one-hour clips** of prison CCTV footage from the Metropolitan Correctional Center. Of the **11 cameras** in the Special Housing Unit where Epstein was held, **10 were not recording** due to malfunctioning hard drives. The single working camera showed only a narrow sliver of the staircase leading to Epstein's tier.

The reconstructed timeline reveals a cascade of failures. On **August 8**, Epstein signed a **pour-over will** transferring all assets — more than **$577 million** — into a private trust called "The 1953 Trust," with lawyer Darren Indyke and accountant Richard Kahn as executors. On **August 9**, his cellmate Efrain Reyes was transferred out with no replacement assigned, despite the requirement. That evening, Epstein made an **unauthorized, unmonitored phone call** — telling staff he wanted to "call his mother," though she had died in 2003. His lawyers described him as *"upbeat"* after their meeting. At approximately 10:30 PM, guard Tova Noel briefly walked by his cell — the last confirmed guard entry to the tier.

**At 10:39 PM, an "orange-colored figure"** was captured on video moving up the staircase toward Epstein's locked tier. The FBI memorandum described it as *"possibly an inmate."* The DOJ Inspector General identified it as an *"unidentified corrections officer"* carrying orange linen. CBS News's independent video analysts said the movement was *"more consistent with an inmate — or someone wearing an orange prison uniform — than a corrections officer."* Guard Noel told investigators she **never distributed linen**. Guard Ghitto Bonhomme — whose identity CBS was first to publicly report — said he could not remember the period between 10 PM and midnight. Neither officer was specifically asked about the orange figure. This occurred within the estimated window of Epstein's possible time of death.

Guards Thomas and Noel **failed to conduct required 30-minute checks** throughout the night, allegedly sleeping and browsing the internet. They failed mandatory counts at **3 AM and 5 AM**. At approximately 6:30 AM on August 10, officer Michael Thomas discovered Epstein suspended from the top bunk by a homemade noose, legs straight out, buttocks roughly 1 to 1.5 inches off the floor. CBS exclusively obtained internal Bureau of Prisons memos describing Epstein as **"cold" with "no palpable pulses."** FBI agents did not arrive until **1:35 PM** — more than seven hours after the body was found. Senior members of Attorney General Barr's staff arrived at MCC, described by a CBS source as *"highly unusual"*; one source said in 20-plus years they had never seen AG senior staff investigate an inmate death.

## A draft death announcement written one day too early

Among the most troubling anomalies CBS identified was a **DOJ draft statement dated August 9, 2019** — one day before Epstein was officially found dead. The draft read: *"Earlier this morning, the Manhattan Correctional Center confirmed that Jeffrey Epstein… had been found unresponsive in his cell and pronounced dead shortly thereafter."* At least two drafts carry the August 9 date; the majority are dated August 10. The early drafts make **no mention of suicide**. The DOJ called it a *"typographical error."* No definitive explanation has been provided.

The autopsy findings remain contested. NYC Chief Medical Examiner Dr. Barbara Sampson ruled suicide by hanging after a four-hour autopsy. But Dr. Michael Baden, the forensic pathologist hired by Epstein's brother who observed the autopsy, told CBS's 60 Minutes he found **three fractures** — left and right thyroid cartilage and the left hyoid bone — and had *"never seen three fractures like this in a suicidal hanging"* across more than a thousand jail hangings over 40-50 years. The **ligature furrow mark** was in the middle of Epstein's neck rather than beneath the jawbone as expected in hanging. The noose depicted in the autopsy *"doesn't match the ligature furrow mark"* — it was wider. No blood transfer appeared on the cloth. **Petechiae** (burst capillaries) on the face, mouth, and eyes were more consistent with strangulation. Baden concluded the evidence pointed *"much more to murder and strangulation than suicide."* The Medical Examiner's office stands firmly behind its ruling.

CBS also resolved one mystery while deepening others. The "missing minute" — a gap from 11:58:58 to 12:00:00 in the FBI's released video — was confirmed by CBS's government sources to be an artifact of the FBI's screen-capture reconstruction process; the original contained no gap. But that raised a new question: the FBI had **destroyed its master copy** of the video (evidence item 1B60) in June 2024, describing it as *"no longer pertinent,"* then had to reconstruct it in mid-2025. Cell scene photographs showed **no evidence markers**, items moved between photos, at least two different nooses found in different rooms, and no fingerprint or DNA tests conducted on anything in the cell. Former NYPD detective Herman Weisberg described the scene as *"staged a bit."* CBS's forensic analyst called it *"an absence of work."*

CBS News has consistently stated that nothing in its review *"suggests foul play,"* but emphasized that the questions are about the **thoroughness and credibility of the investigation** rather than the conclusion itself.

## How CBS built its database and what remains hidden

CBS News uploaded all DOJ releases to **Google Journalist Studio's Pinpoint platform**, providing OCR-searchable access across mixed PDF folders filterable by Data Set and document type. The newsroom reviewed files in collaboration with NBC, Versant, and The Associated Press, with each outlet responsible for independent coverage. CBS's verification method was rigorous: the team downloaded the complete initial release on December 19, 2025, compared it to what was available the following day, and thereby identified the 15 disappearing files. **The Free Press** (also owned by Paramount/Skydance) watched all video in Data Set 10 and produced a compilation excluding duplicates, audio-only files, and fully redacted content — yielding approximately **14 hours of footage** including the Steve Bannon-Epstein interview, the 46-minute FBI sting of butler Alfredo Rodriguez selling the "little black book," explicit self-recorded videos, and clips of young women in various states of undress with faces obscured by DOJ black boxes.

CBS's most significant exclusive discoveries include the DEA narcotics/money-laundering investigation, the Barrack-Epstein correspondence, the Lutnick business documents, the identification of guard Bonhomme, the orange-figure video analysis, and the cell-scene evidence-handling failures. The network also reported that **Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem**, CEO of Dubai's DP World, appeared more than **4,700 times** in the files — leading to his replacement at DP World — and that former UK Ambassador **Peter Mandelson** was arrested after files showed he shared confidential government information with Epstein.

## Conclusion

The CBS News Epstein Files investigation reveals a case defined not just by what the documents contain, but by what remains missing. Approximately half the estimated 6 million qualifying pages remain unreleased. FBI 302 witness interview statements are still largely withheld. The DEA, ICE, and FBI ran parallel investigations into Epstein's financial and trafficking operations without apparent coordination — and without prosecution. The surveillance system monitoring the highest-profile federal detainee in the country suffered cascading failures at precisely the moment it mattered most. A draft death announcement appeared one day early. An orange figure entered a locked tier at 10:39 PM and has never been identified. Three bone fractures defied the experience of a forensic pathologist who had examined a thousand jail hangings. And 38,000 references to a sitting president sit in a document archive controlled by his own Justice Department. CBS News has been methodical in documenting these facts without drawing conspiratorial conclusions — but the facts themselves constitute an extraordinary record of institutional failure, from the 1996 FBI complaint that went uninvestigated for a decade to the 2007 indictment that was never filed, to the 2019 death that may never be fully explained.
